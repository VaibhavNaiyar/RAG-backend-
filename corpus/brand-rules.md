# Brand & Claims Rules — Marketing (DEMO)

> Written for this demo. A simplified internal policy, not legal advice and not
> any real company's policy. Real copy goes to a human compliance reviewer.

## BR-1 Approval status of compounded medication

Never describe a compounded medication as "FDA-approved", "FDA-cleared" or
"the same as Wegovy" or "the same as Zepbound". Compounded drugs are not
FDA-approved. Permitted wording: "compounded semaglutide, prescribed by a
licensed provider when clinically appropriate".

## BR-2 Brand names belong to the approved product

Do not use the trademarked names Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound or Mounjaro in ads
for compounded products. Brand names may only be used when the advertised
product is the FDA-approved branded product itself.

## BR-3 No guaranteed results

Never promise a specific outcome ("lose 20 lbs in a month", "guaranteed weight
loss", "results or your money back on weight"). Outcome numbers may only be
quoted from an FDA label's clinical studies section, attributed to the study,
and paired with the phrase "individual results vary".

## BR-4 "Clinically proven" and other establishment claims

"Clinically proven", "doctor recommended" and "science-backed" are
establishment claims. They are allowed only for the FDA-approved product they
were proven for, citing its label. Never use them for a compounded product.

## BR-5 Safety information travels with benefit claims

Any ad that makes a benefit claim about a GLP-1 medication must include, or
link prominently to, important safety information, including the boxed warning
about the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors. Safety information may not be hidden
in small print or behind an unlabeled link.

## BR-6 Testimonials and before/after images

Testimonials and before/after photos must reflect typical results, must be from
real patients with written consent, and must carry "individual results vary".
Paid or incentivized testimonials must disclose the payment clearly.

## BR-7 Prescription required

Ads must make clear the medication requires a prescription and a consultation
with a licensed provider. Never imply guaranteed approval ("get your
prescription today, no questions asked").

## BR-8 Price and urgency claims

Prices must include what is and is not included (consultation, medication,
shipping). No fake scarcity ("only 3 spots left") unless it is literally true
and documented.

## BR-9 Escalation

If a claim is not clearly covered by these rules or the cited regulatory
guidance, the answer is "needs review" and the copy goes to the compliance
team. When in doubt, escalate.
